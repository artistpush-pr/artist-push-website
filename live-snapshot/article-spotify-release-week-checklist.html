Spotify Release Week Checklist: 14 Days Before, 3 Days After

Most release plans fail on a date, not on a decision. In this entire process there is one date that cannot be shifted retroactively, and everything else in this checklist is aligned around it.

Most release plans fail on a date, not on a decision. In this entire process there is one date that cannot be shifted retroactively, and everything else in this checklist is aligned around it.

Countdown format. All of these dates assume Friday as a default release day.

Minus 14 days: time-sensitive action items

Verify pitch eligibility. You need Admin or Editor permissions on your artist profile in Spotify for Artists. Your profile may be managed by a distributor, in which case you can't log in at all — and realising that six days out is by far the most common cause of self-sabotage in this whole process.

Pitch the track. This item has a hard deadline. Spotify requires at least 7 days before release, but 2–4 weeks is the working range. Editors have a backlog of songs to listen to, and the earlier your track enters the queue, the more listening slots it can fall into. The requirements are restrictive: one song at a time, unreleased only, no compilations, and no featured appearances.

The pitch form is the area where the biggest differences are made — genre and mood tagging, instruments, language and city, and a description that you shouldn't skip. We explain what to include in the pitch in its own article.

Contact independent curators. They take 5–14 days to process, and starting now means placements land in release week rather than a month later when nobody cares anymore.

Create a Countdown Page for albums and EPs. It lets fans pre-save the release, view the tracklist and watch countdown videos. Pre-saves matter because they put the release into a fan's own library — which counts as an active source rather than a programmed one.

Optimise your profile. Artist image, bio, Artist Pick. Everything from here on sends people to a page, and that page either converts them or it doesn't.

Minus 7 days: the floor

This is the last day the pitch is possible. Miss it and you lose two things: consideration for editorial playlists on this release, and the automatic placement of the song in your own followers' Release Radar.

There's one catch to the second part. As of 10 July 2026, listeners can customise their Release Radar — limiting it to a single genre, to new-to-them artists only, or through a set of options including *Discover new artists* and *Editors' picks*. So what's guaranteed is a slot in a feed the listener can filter, not that anyone hears it. This cuts both ways: for smaller artists, the *Discover new artists* filter is the first personalised feature Spotify has shipped that specifically helps unknown artists get found.

Minus 3 to minus 1 days: the quick things you might skip

Add a Canvas to the track. It's free, it's per-track, and it's the difference between a static square and something that holds the attention of a listener who isn't actively engaged.

Select the Countdown Page or pre-save link for the Artist Pick.

Ensure all your links go to the appropriate destination. A link from a video, a social media post or an email that goes to your artist profile or release page produces what Spotify categorises as an *active* listener. The same person arriving through a programmed playlist doesn't. How you direct people to the release determines how Spotify classifies them.

Set your budget and choose one channel. Trying to stretch a small budget across pitching, ads and placements will dilute all your data and make it difficult to discern results.

Release day: Friday

Release Radar will fire for your followers if you pitched inside the window. There's no algorithmic action for you to worry about today, since Discover Weekly only updates on Monday.

Two rules for the day itself.

Don't evaluate any metrics. It's too early to measure the success of your release. Streams don't begin counting until after 30 seconds, and playlist placements haven't started reporting.

Send people to your release page, not a playlist. As above: you're choosing how Spotify categorises your first listeners.

Plus 1 to 3: reading it right

There is a brief period here where things move fast enough to be read in real time, and this is where most artists look at the wrong number.

Look at save rate, not stream count. A save puts your song in a library, and a library is an active source — the only action that gives a listener a way back to you once the placement is over. Stream count on day two shows how much exposure you bought. Save rate shows whether any of it stuck. Read the whole argument for what numbers actually matter before jumping to conclusions off a chart.

Watch the source-of-streams split. Rising streams with a falling active share means a placement is holding the release up, and it will stop holding it up when the placement stops.

Expect curator placements to start coming in. If you reached out two weeks in advance, this is when the 5–14 day windows begin resolving.

Don't rush to buy volume. A slow day two makes it tempting to buy streams, and it ruins the very ratio you're trying to read: purchased streams with no saves behind them make a track look like something plenty of people heard and nobody wanted. There's also the risk noted elsewhere in this blog — since 1 April 2024, Spotify charges distributors about €10 per track where it spots blatant artificial streaming, and that charge reaches the artist.

After day plus 3

Monthly listeners will lag. It's a 28-day rolling window, so it keeps going up for weeks after release-week activity is over, then drops when the window empties. Don't treat it as a live read.

Look at Discovery Mode once you have a signal. There's no upfront cost, and you give up 30% of the royalty on streams it drives through Radio and Autoplay. It boosts a signal, it doesn't create one — enrolling a track nobody listens to past the 30-second mark just wastes the discount.

Look at display campaigns if you qualify. Marquee is the full-screen callout on Home for a new release, running about ten days and starting around $100. Showcase is the mobile banner, running about fourteen days and working on back catalogue too.

Bear in mind the royalty minimum. A song earns nothing until it passes 1,000 streams, which is worth knowing before you work out whether any of this paid for itself.

Where paid promotion fits

If you're going to spend, spend during release week, not afterwards. That's when engagement compounds into algorithmic reach, rather than arriving late, after the systems have already made their minds up.

We do sell promotion, so take that into account when weighing the advice here: our packages are designed specifically for this window, and the honest note is the same one that runs through this whole blog. Paid promotion only amplifies what a track is already doing to its listeners. If it has a save rate worth amplifying, this is the most valuable moment there is to spend. If it doesn't, that same spend generates a blip that leaves the 28-day window and takes nothing with it.

Unromantic as it sounds, the right sequence is this: pitch first because it's free, get real people listening and engaging, and then consider paying for reach for something that's already shown it can hold someone.

FAQ

Is the release day supposed to be Friday?

Not necessarily, but Release Radar updates on Fridays and the industry operates on that schedule. Releasing on a different day forfeits that alignment for no clear benefit.

Can I submit a song that's already released?

No. The pitch form requires an unreleased song. It's better to work on the next single than to try relaunching a previous one.

How soon will I find out if an editor chose my track?

There's no notification either way. You find out by looking at where your streams come from in Spotify for Artists over the first few days after release.

Should I put out singles or the full EP at once?

With singles you get more pitches — one per song, and the clock resets each time. With an EP you get a Countdown Page and one bigger moment. Independent artists tend to benefit more from a release cadence than from a single large drop.

What happens if I miss the seven-day deadline?

You lose editorial consideration for that release and the automatic Release Radar placement, and you can't pitch the song once it's live. Independent curators are unaffected — they don't work to Spotify's deadline. The track can still be picked up algorithmically later. Give the next release more lead time.

What should I budget for release week?

Nothing, if you're only pitching — submitting is free. Any spend beyond that depends on whether the song's save rate is high enough to justify amplifying it.
