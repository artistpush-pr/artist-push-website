What's actually happening when someone reposts your song

They're putting your song into the Following feed of every user who follows the account that reposted it. That's all the mechanic is. There's no placement, no submission form, no curation panel, no application — just one button, and suddenly your track is in the feed of a whole new audience.

That's why reposts, not playlists, are the currency on SoundCloud. You're not looking to get added to a static list of songs. You're looking to have your song passed down the line through multiple Following feeds. For the rest of what's going on with SoundCloud as a platform there's a separate piece — this article is all about that one button.

Here are two details from SoundCloud's own docs that most articles don't mention:

SoundCloud's Following feed is ordered by time. It's not ranked. Which means timing is more important than it is in a ranked feed. Your repost will be up against other tracks posted in the same hour.

SoundCloud lets you add a caption to a repost, and describes captions as a way to make a post stand out in the Following tab. The reposter's caption is visible to their followers. A repost with a sentence attached is an endorsement; a bare repost is a shrug.

The math-changing rule

Almost nobody selling reposts will share this part, and it's taken directly from the SoundCloud help page: there will only ever be one post or repost of the same track in your Following feed.

If multiple accounts you follow have reposted the same track, you don't see that track multiple times. The most recent repost is shown at the top of your feed, and it erases the earlier entry for that same track further down.

Let's break this down for a repost campaign. Say 5 channels repost your track, and their audiences overlap heavily. This is what happens inside a repost network, because many of the listeners in these networks follow a lot of the member channels. In that scenario, a listener who follows all 5 of those channels sees your track once. You paid for 5. They gave you 1 impression.

So the value of a repost campaign isn't the number of reposts. It's the unique, non-overlapping audience size behind the accounts that shared your track. 5 channels with entirely different followings are much more valuable than 50 channels drawn from the same audience pool.

This single rule helps explain why many large repost packages under-deliver compared to their listed numbers. The only question you need to ask sellers is: whose audiences, and how much do they overlap?

What people actually hear

When someone sees your track in their feed, they don't hear the track. They hear a thirty-second preview.

SoundCloud allows you to select the preview yourself, when you're uploading a track, in the Advanced section. If you don't, SoundCloud's AI selects a thirty-second excerpt for you. This is also what gets used if the track is shared to a social network.

It seems like almost nobody uses this feature — which means a machine has chosen the first thirty seconds of you that a stranger will ever hear, at the precise moment a repost has bought you their attention. Doing it yourself costs nothing and takes seconds. It might be the most important thirty seconds on your entire profile.

A couple more points from the same section of the docs: the image you upload is displayed in the feed, and if you didn't upload one, SoundCloud displays your profile picture instead. And a private track doesn't show up in the feed at all.

How to get reposts that matter

Ask people who have your audience, not people who have many followers. A repost only matters if the people it reaches would plausibly enjoy your music. Genre overlap wins every time, and the deduplication rule above means the second outlet in the same scene is often a much smaller addition than it looks like.

Repost other people properly. Repost-for-repost is the oldest form of currency on SoundCloud, but it only works if you're an account whose reposts people trust. If you repost everything, your reposts mean nothing to your own followers.

Ask for a caption. If someone's going to repost you, a sentence from them counts for more than the repost alone. It's also a simple request to make.

Keep an eye on timing. Reverse chronological means a repost that lands when that audience is inactive is effectively invisible.

Make sure it's something worth reposting. Edits, remixes, unreleased tracks and special versions tend to circulate more than a normal single, because reposting them says something about the reposter's taste.

Repost packages

Since we sell them, we'll frame this carefully: these repost packages are on our SoundCloud page, and here is an honest description of what you're actually getting.

A bought repost is a repost — the button is pressed, the song appears in the feed of those followers. What you're getting is exposure to an audience, not an endorsement from someone who picked your song. The audience is as real as the channel's audience is, and that varies greatly in this market.

Three caveats when considering buying any repost package, from anyone.

How much overlap is there? A package with a large number of reposts across a network is limited by how many listeners those channels share. Get the names of the channels, then see if they're all the same scene, all talking to the same listeners.

Repost networks are artist networks. A lot of repost channels have a high percentage of followers who are other artists looking for reposts, not fans looking for music. Plays from those accounts behave differently than plays from actual fans, and they don't turn into the listening that pays out on SoundCloud.

A repost is a moment, not a home. It shows up in the feed, gets scrolled past or played, and it's done. It doesn't sit somewhere accumulating attention the way a playlist slot would. Anything left after that moment has to be produced by the track itself, in those thirty seconds.

If you're comparing this to buying plays or likes, the reasoning about what platforms count as fake activity applies here too — though reposts are easier to defend than play counts, since they show up as an account doing something it might do anyway, rather than a stream from nowhere. It's still a question of degree, not a magic solution.

FAQ

Will a repost show on my profile?

Reposts will be visible on your profile under the Activity section, and visible to your followers. Because reposts are public, an account that reposts randomly loses influence.

Can I remove a repost?

Yes, by un-reposting. It disappears from the feeds it went out on going forward, but anyone who has already played the song has already played it.

Do reposts count as plays?

No. A repost is a distribution action. It only becomes a play if someone clicks the preview.

How many reposts do I need?

The wrong question, considering deduplication. What you want is a set of reposting accounts whose audiences hardly overlap and genuinely match your sound. 3 of them will outweigh 30 of the other kind.

Do reposts help with getting on the SoundCloud Discover tab?

Indirectly. Discover is built on how similar a track is to what a listener already plays and likes. A repost's main function is to create the listening that Discover recommendations are calculated from.

Can I repost my own track from a second account?

It would just go out to that account's followers. If that account doesn't have a distinct following, it won't accomplish anything except making both profiles look thinner.
