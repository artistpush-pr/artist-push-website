A lot of advice on SoundCloud is just advice on Spotify with the names swapped, and it doesn't work for one structural reason: SoundCloud is a social network that also has a music player inside it. There are no editorial playlists to pitch. There's no Discover Weekly. What there is instead is a feed, a repost button, and a comment field that lives next to the waveform.

That alters what "promoting" even means here. On Spotify you're aiming to get placed. On SoundCloud you're aiming to get shared.

What actually changed heading into 2026

Just two things, and both matter if you're considering spending any money here.

The tiers got new names and prices. The old Next Pro is dead. There are now Artist for $3.25/month (billed annually) and Artist Pro for $8.25/month (billed annually). Artist gives you a 3-hour upload cap, 3 replaceable tracks a month, 1 mastering credit, and distribution and monetization for 2 tracks a month to 50+ stores and platforms. Artist Pro lifts the caps: unlimited uploads, unlimited distribution and monetization, 3 mastering credits, advanced audience insights, a comments hub, scheduled releases, and split royalties. Any guide still telling you to purchase Next Pro was published before those changes and likely contains other outdated information.

Distribution took a 0% cut. At the end of November 2025, SoundCloud eliminated its distribution revenue share, and both current plans state they offer 100% royalties. This is relevant because it changes the math for using SoundCloud as your distributor, not just as a place to upload: everything that Spotify, Apple Music, TikTok and the rest send you, you retain.

The mechanic that runs the platform

Reposts. Not playlists, not the algorithm, not follower count.

A repost puts your track onto someone else's followers' timelines, meaning one decent channel with a real audience can move a track further than a whole month of uploads. This is why the SoundCloud economy has always revolved around repost channels and taste-maker accounts, not playlist curators. It is also the mechanic that is most misunderstood and worst-sold, which is why it's worth knowing what a repost actually does before you buy one.

The practical takeaway for promotion: you aren't optimising for a spot that stays put. You're optimising for a spot that spreads. A track that is reposted by an account whose followers also repost it is travelling; a track sitting on a massive channel that nobody interacts with isn't.

Comments are a real signal, and the only one you can't easily spoof

SoundCloud comments are time-stamped and pinned to the waveform. They are public, they are seen by all listeners, and they're tied to a specific moment in the track.

That makes them the most valuable form of engagement on SoundCloud and the most dangerous to inflate. A bought comment looks like a bought comment. The same three words at the three-second mark on every track from every artist is a pattern that needs no detection system at all. Real comments refer to the drop, the lyric, the sample, the hook. You don't need an algorithm to tell the difference — and neither does the A&R scrolling through your page.

If you nail just one type of engagement on SoundCloud, make it this one.

First Fans and how discovery actually happens

The thing on SoundCloud designed specifically to surface fresh uploads to likely listeners is called First Fans. It uses AI from Musiio, a company SoundCloud acquired, which analyses the audio itself and then serves it to people whose listening history indicates they'll enjoy it. Each new upload gets surfaced to about 100 relevant listeners through autoplay. Priority goes to paid Pro accounts.

There are two important limitations. One hundred listeners is a start, not a marketing campaign. And a recommendation is not a play — the system can put you in front of someone, but it cannot force them to press play or finish your song. What it does provide is a brand-new track's first non-zero data, which is what everything downstream needs in order to work at all.

This makes a smaller audience more valuable on SoundCloud

SoundCloud's Fan-Powered Royalties model pays out based on what each listener actually streams. Rather than having all subscriber revenue pooled and divided by overall streams, each listener's payment goes only to the artists they actually listened to. There is no minimum number of streams required to start earning once your tracks are monetized.

The upside is that a hundred people who listen to you constantly are worth much more here than the same hundred spread across a pro-rata platform, because those listeners' subscription fees are directed toward you instead of being added to a collective pool distributed among the largest artists on the service.

This is a strong reason to treat SoundCloud as a worthwhile platform, not just a place to dump leftover material. It's also why buying volume here is self-defeating in a way that goes beyond policy: Fan-Powered Royalties is a model where the identity of your listeners matters. A manufactured listener has no subscription fee to share.

What works

Upload on a schedule. The feed rewards presence. Irregular posting will make your followers assume you have disappeared.

Add a title, image and description to every single song. Search on SoundCloud is text-based and extremely literal. `Master_final_v3` is impossible to find.

Use tags. They are still an important tool on SoundCloud, and most people don't use them properly because they seem outdated.

Reply to comments. SoundCloud is a social platform, after all. Replies bring the song back onto followers' timelines and convert passive listeners into returning ones.

Post early releases, remixes and works in progress. SoundCloud is one of the only platforms where unfinished music is considered acceptable, and those are often the tracks that get shared.

Focus on having 2–3 channel relationships instead of 20. A repost from a smaller account with a relevant audience is more valuable than one from a larger account that doesn't resonate with your sound.

Enable monetisation if you're on a paid plan. Fan-Powered Royalties can't pay you for listening you never switched on.

FAQ

Will SoundCloud be relevant in 2026?

Yes, for certain audiences and for discovery, especially for electronic, hip-hop and anything that relies on edits, remixes and unreleased material. With the distribution cut removed it has also become a legitimate distributor rather than just a posting platform.

Do I have to pay to get any value from SoundCloud promotion?

To post, no. To have access to the useful tools, yes. First Fans priority, scheduled uploads, unlimited monetization and actual analytics are all paid-tier features. At $39 a year for the entry plan, it mostly depends on how regularly you upload.

What follower count do I need to make reposts work for me?

A lot less than you might think, because reposts don't rely on your own following. They rely on the match between your track and the people following the account that reposts it.

Should I delete old tracks with low play counts?

Mostly no. A SoundCloud profile is a catalogue people browse, and having a backlog of edits and demos is what makes a profile look like an artist's rather than a release diary.

Do my SoundCloud plays translate into Spotify streams?

No, they're different platforms with different metrics. What does carry over is attention — people finding you on SoundCloud and then going looking for you elsewhere.
